You are given several specifications of the same feature, written independently by different people at
different times. The project kept all of them. Produce **one document** that consolidates them.

This is a consolidation, **not** a rewrite and **not** a summary. The job is to make the pile readable
as a single document while changing nothing about what it says.

## Keep everything

- **Every statement in every source survives.** If a source says something in a roundabout way, keep the
  roundabout way. If two sources say the same thing differently, keep both phrasings — they came from
  different authors and each may have meant something slightly different.
- **Where the sources disagree, keep the disagreement.** Do not average, do not pick a winner, do not
  add a note saying which is probably right. Put the conflicting statements near each other and leave
  them there. Deciding is not your job on this pass.
- **Where the sources are silent or vague about something, stay silent or vague.** Do not fill a gap, do
  not infer the obvious answer, do not add a sentence beginning "presumably" or "by default". If you
  find yourself about to decide something none of the sources decided, stop and leave it undecided.
- Keep every worked example, every restatement, every section that describes the document rather than
  the feature.

## What you may change

Only presentation:

- one set of section headings instead of six
- statements about the same topic grouped together under the same heading
- consistent terminology where the sources plainly mean the same thing by different words
- transitions so the document reads continuously rather than as six stacked files

## Output

Markdown only, starting with the first heading. No preamble, no closing commentary, no note about what
you merged or what you noticed. Do not add a "summary", a "key decisions" section, or a table of
contents that states conclusions the sources did not state.
